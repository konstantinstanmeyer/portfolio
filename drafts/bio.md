# Bio

The About text from the home page (`app/page.tsx`), set aside on 2026-11-06 for lorem ipsum. To restore it, put each paragraph back in its own `<p>` inside the About section's `s.text` div.

The part of this work that matters most happens after it ships, for people who will never know who built it: someone finding the hours on a slow phone, booking on the first try, reading the menu with a screen reader, then getting on with their day. The business behind it should be able to afford that, and to keep it running without calling anyone.

Better tools have made that easier to give. The routine parts of programming and design take minutes now instead of days, leaving the time for what still needs a person: deciding what’s worth building, who it’s for, and making sure everyone can use it. Accessibility used to be the first thing cut when time or money ran short; now there’s room for it. At Myrtle Street Media, that means websites for local businesses that cost less, do more, and work for anyone who visits them.

Before the code came photographs, mostly of strangers, mostly in the second after they forgot about the camera: good practice, it turns out, for building things meant to be used without a second thought. Based in Boston.
